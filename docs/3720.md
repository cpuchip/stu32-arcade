# 3720: fly through the rocks (blueprint)

Michael's ask (abacus #10254, keys #10256 and #10265): a rock-field shooter for a young player who doesn't read yet,
with a gentle mode as the default and the full game as a second mode. The name, 3720, is Michael's. The form (a ship that turns and
thrusts in a wrap-around field and breaks drifting rocks into smaller ones) is an old arcade form; its best-known
title is a trademark, so nothing here uses it. The rules, the numbers, the rock outlines and every sprite below are
ours. This is the design and the checks that will judge it, written before any of its code.

The ship's turn, its integer physics and its outline drawing come from LAND (docs/land.md), so the two games feel
the same in the hand and share one sine table's numbers.

## A game

The ship starts in the middle of the screen. Large rocks drift in from the edges. Shots break a rock into two
smaller ones, and the smallest kind breaks into nothing. Everything wraps: what leaves one edge comes back at the
opposite one. Clearing every rock ends the wave, and the next one drifts in. A rock that touches the ship destroys
it, and the ship comes back in the middle behind a short shield.

- **Gentle (the default):** three or four large, slow rocks that split once. Ships lost in the first minute of a game
  cost nothing, so the game can't end before then. The rocks never speed up.
- **Full:** three sizes, more rocks each wave (to 6 large), faster each wave, a numeric score and extra ships.

| key | does |
|---|---|
| 4 or SOFT1 | turns the nose 15° to the left, and again every 100 ms while held |
| 6 or SOFT6 | the same to the right |
| 7, 8, 9 or SOFT2 | fires the engine while held |
| GOLD, 5, × or SOFT5 | fires while held (a shot as often as the rules allow) |
| 4/SOFT1 and 6/SOFT6 on the title | pick gentle (left picture) or full (right picture) |
| ENTER | starts a game from the title, or a new one after the end |
| C | the framework's: saves and ends the game |

The keys are Michael's (#10256, #10265): "4/6 turn still makes sense / But gold and x can be fire / And 7,8,9 are
thruster", then "5 should also fire. Gold, 5, x". So the thrust keys are the row above the fire keys, and the turn
keys sit between the fire keys. On the soft row (Michael, abacus #10280), SOFT1 and SOFT6 turn as in BABAL, LAND and
SALVO, SOFT2 thrusts and SOFT5 fires, so a player on that row has all three jobs. ENTER only starts a game. Turn, fire
and thrust have to work held at once, three keys together; the rollover measurement is under "On the board".

## The rules (all integers)

**Units.** As in LAND: positions in 1/65,536 px (`Q` = 65,536), speeds in Q a step. The field is 400 × 228 px,
drawn under a 12 px top line, so a field y of 0 is screen row 12. x wraps mod 400 px and y mod 228 px.

**The clock.** SALVO's: a step every 50 ms; a tick adds its ms, at most 100, to an accumulator; while it holds 50 a
step runs and 50 is taken off, so a tick gives at most two steps. A game starts on ENTER, and its first tick counts
only the time since that key.

**The keys in play.** A bit for each key (LAND's mask): a down sets it and latches its job, an up clears it, so a tap
shorter than a step still counts once. Left is any left key held or latched, right the same, thrust any thrust key,
fire any fire key. The thrust and fire latches clear after each step. The turn latches are LAND's presses: they stay
until a turn step uses them (below), so a tap during the turn timer's count still turns once the timer is 0; they
clear on any step the ship is not in play, judged at the step's start: the step it is lost keeps them, the step it
returns clears them. A key already down on the title counts for nothing until it is pressed
again.

**The draws.** Each game has a state `x = mix64(seed ^ mix64(game))` (SplitMix64's finalizer, the mix64 of
Aim, Land and Salvo; `seed = app_seed()`, used as it is, `game` the count of games started before this one, 0
for the first, kept in the save), and `draw(n)` is `x = mix64(x)` then `x % n`. Every draw comes from this
one state in the order the rules make them; nothing else in the game is random.
`mix64(z)`, all mod 2^64: `z += 0x9e3779b97f4a7c15; z = (z ^ (z >> 30)) × 0xbf58476d1ce4e5b9; z = (z ^ (z >> 27)) ×
0x94d049bb133111eb; return z ^ (z >> 31)`.

**Angles.** `a` is 0-23 in 15° steps, 0 pointing up, increasing clockwise; `SIN[a]` and `COS[a]` are LAND's
(round(16,384 × sin(a × 15°)) and the same for cos).

**The ship.** A centre (x, y), a speed (vx, vy) and an angle a; a game starts at (200, 114) px, still, a = 0.
- **Turn.** LAND's rule at this clock: the turn timer counts down by 1 if above 0; at 0, right − left (each 0 or 1)
  turns the ship by it, mod 24, the timer becomes 2 and both turn latches clear. Held, a turn repeats every 2 steps
  (100 ms); a tap turns once. Both together cancel and clear.
- **Thrust.** `vx += T × SIN[a] / 16,384`, `vy −= T × COS[a] / 16,384` (C's `/`).
- **Drag**, every step, thrust or not: `vx −= vx / 32`, `vy −= vy / 32`.
- **The cap:** vx and vy clamped to ±V.
- Then `x += vx`, `y += vy`, each wrapped.

| | gentle | full |
|---|---|---|
| T, thrust a step | 3,277 (0.05 px) | 6,554 (0.1 px) |
| V, the speed cap | 131,072 (2 px a step) | 262,144 (4 px) |
| shots at once | 3 | 4 |
| shot cooldown | 4 steps | 3 steps |
| shot life | 20 steps | 16 steps |
| ships | 3, free in the first minute | 3, plus one each 5,000 points, at most 5 |
| shield after a lost ship, and at the start | 60 steps (3 s) | 40 steps (2 s) |
| rock sizes (radius) | large 24 px, medium 12 px | large 20, medium 10, small 5 |
| large rocks a wave | 3 in wave 1, then 4 | min(6, 3 + wave) |

**Shots.** A cooldown counts down by 1 each step to 0. When the ship is in play, fire is on, the cooldown is 0 and
fewer than the limit fly, a shot appears at the ship's nose, its centre plus (32 × SIN[a], −32 × COS[a]) Q (8 px along
a), with the speed (28 × SIN[a], −28 × COS[a]) Q (7 px a step) plus the ship's (vx, vy), and the cooldown is set. A shot wraps like everything
else and is gone after its life. A shot moves in two halves, each half tested against the rocks (the hits, below),
so a shot can't step over a small rock.

**Rocks.** 24 slots, each empty or holding a rock: centre, speed, size and outline (0-3). Each large rock of a wave
accounts for at most four rocks alive at once (two medium, each two small), and a wave has at most 6 large, so 24
slots always hold every rock.
- **A wave's rocks**, for each large rock in turn: heading `h = draw(24)`, speed `s` (below), outline `draw(4)`,
  x = `draw(400)` px, y = 0 (the field's top and bottom seam). If its distance to the ship (below) is under 80 px
  plus its radius, x moves 200 px (mod 400). Speed is `(s × SIN[h] / 16,384, −s × COS[h] / 16,384)`.
- **Speeds**, s in Q a step: gentle large `16,384 + draw(16,385)` (0.25-0.5 px), gentle medium
  `26,214 + draw(19,661)` (0.4-0.7 px). Full: a base `b = 32,768 + 3,277 × min(wave − 1, 10)` (0.5 px, rising to
  1.0), large `b + draw(b / 2)`, medium `3b / 2 + draw(b / 2)`, small `2b + draw(b / 2)`.
- **A split.** A hit rock of a size that splits leaves two of the next size at its centre: the first in its slot,
  the second in the first empty slot. The first takes `h1 = draw(24)`, the second `h1 + 12` (mod 24, the opposite
  way), then each its speed and outline, in that order. Gentle's medium and full's small don't split; a hit removes
  them.
- Each step a rock moves by its speed and wraps.

**The distance.** Between two centres, each difference wraps to the nearest way round (dx into −200 px ≤ dx < 200 px,
dy into −114 px ≤ dy < 114 px), then becomes 1/256 px (C's `/ 256`); the test is `dx² + dy² < r²` in 64 bits, with r
in 1/256 px.

**One step**, in this order:
1. the ship turns, thrusts, drags and moves (if it is in play);
2. the rocks move, by slot order;
3. the shots: each, oldest first, moves its first half and is tested, then its second half and is tested; then one
   may fire (it is first tested on its next step's first half);
4. the ship against the rocks;
5. the timers: cooldown, shield, the lost ship's count, the wave's pause, the game's play clock.

**The hits.**
1. A shot against the rocks in slot order: the first rock whose radius covers the shot's point (the distance to its
   centre under r) breaks, and the shot is gone. Rocks made by that break can be hit by later shots in the same
   step. Score: gentle counts one mark a rock, any size; full scores 10, 25, 50 (large, medium, small).
2. When the ship is in play and its shield is 0: the first rock in slot order within r + 6 px of its centre breaks
   (scoring nothing) and the ship is lost.

**A lost ship.** Its burst shows for 20 steps (1 s) and the rocks keep moving. In gentle, a ship lost before the
game's play clock reaches 1,200 steps (60 s, counted while the ship is in play) costs nothing. Otherwise the ships
go down by 1, and with none left the game is over after the burst. Else the ship returns at (200, 114), still, a =
0, with the shield. While shielded it flies and fires and rocks pass through it.

**The wave's end.** When no rock is left: every shot is gone, the rising tune plays, 40 steps pass (2 s), and the
next wave comes (wave + 1). A ship lost on the step that clears a wave is lost as above, and the pause starts from
its return.

## The fine print

The model's author read the rules above with no C in sight and listed what they left open (2026-10-24); each answer
below is a rule, and the C and the model both follow it.

1. **The turn.** The timer counts down first, then the test. It becomes 2 only when the ship turns; both turn keys, or
   neither, leave it at 0, and both cancel and clear the turn latches. While the ship is lost the timer stands still,
   and it is not reset when the ship returns.
2. **A shot's halves** are `v / 2` then `v − v / 2` (C's `/`), so the whole move is v; the position wraps after each
   half, and each shot makes both halves before the next shot moves. Its life counts down after its second half (only
   if nothing was hit), and it is gone at 0: a shot reads 20 (gentle) or 16 (full) on the step it fires and appears
   in that many lines.
3. **Firing.** "Fewer than the limit" counts after this step's moves, hits and lives. The new shot takes the angle and
   speed after step 1, and its nose position wraps. The cooldown is set in step 3 and counted in step 5, so it reads
   3 (gentle) or 2 (full) on the firing step, and the ship fires every 4 or 3 steps while held.
4. **A hit** is strictly inside: `dx² + dy² < r²`, for shots and for the ship (r + 6 px).
5. **A split's draws** are h1, the first's speed, the first's outline, the second's speed, the second's outline. With
   no empty slot (possible only from a hand-set state) every draw is still made and the second rock is dropped. Full
   mode's `b` uses the current wave. The two new rocks first move on the next step, but later shots in the same step
   can hit them, in any slot.
6. **A wave's rocks** take the first empty slots in turn; per rock the draws are heading, speed, outline, x. The
   move-off test is strict and uses the ship's centre as it stands. (With the ship at (200, 114), a rock at y 0 is at
   least 114 px away and the threshold is at most 104 px, so it never moves one in wave 1.)
7. **The pause** starts in step 5 when it is 0, no rock is left, the ship is in play and the game isn't over: every
   shot is gone and the pause is 40 (the line shows 40), counting from the next step. Since the burst is counted
   first, a pause can start on the step the ship returns. Shots fired during the pause live on into the next wave.
   The next wave is made in step 5 on the step the pause reaches 0; that line shows its rocks at y 0.
8. **The burst.** `lost` is set to 20 in step 4 and not counted that step, so the line shows 20; the ship returns in
   step 5 twenty steps later, its shield set after the shield's own count (so it reads the full 60 or 40). During the
   burst the ship's place, speed and angle stand still; shots fly and the cooldown counts.
9. **Ships** go down at the loss. The game is over when `lost` reaches 0 with no ships; that line shows `lost` 0, the
   ship where it was, and the pause and clock skipped. An extra ship scored during the burst saves the game.
10. **The play clock** counts in step 5, after the burst's count, when `lost` is 0: not on a loss's step, and on a
    return's step. It counts during pauses and in full too. A loss is free in gentle when the clock read under 1,200
    at step 4 (the value from the step before).
11. **The shield** counts down in step 5 before the burst, while the ship is in play: a game starts at 60, so step 1
    reads 59 and the ship is safe on steps 1 to 60.
12. **Extra ships** are checked whenever a shot scores, as many as the score passed; `next` moves up by 5,000 each
    time, and a ship is added only below 5 (ships counts the one in play, already less one during a burst).
13. **A rock that hits the ship** splits with the same draws as a shot's hit and scores nothing; only the first in
    slot order counts.
14. **Drag and the cap.** The cap comes after drag; a speed under 32 Q in size is left as it is.
15. **Ticks.** After the game is over a tick adds nothing; when a game ends on a tick's first step, the second does
    not run and the accumulator keeps its remainder. `acc` prints after its 50 is taken off.

## The screen (400 × 240, draw.h only)

**No words are needed to play.** What a non-reader sees:
- **The top line, 12 px:** a small ship for each ship held beyond the one in play, at the left. The score at the
  right: in gentle as tally marks (four strokes and a fifth across, a group of five, 10 px), and five groups (25
  rocks) fold into one star, so 63 rocks reads as two stars, two groups and three strokes; at most 30 stars are drawn.
  Full shows its score in digits, since its points (10, 25, 50) don't count as marks.
- **The field:** the ship as a 4-point outline turned to a (nose (0, −8), (6, 7), (0, 3), (−6, 7), LAND's exact
  ×4 turn), with a flame on alternate steps while the engine fires; near an edge it is drawn again, wrapped. The
  shield is a dotted circle of radius 11 px (16 dots), blinking in its last 20 steps. Rocks are outlines of 10
  points at our own radius factors (four outlines, each scaled to the rock's radius: a table of 40 bytes), drawn
  with draw_line, wrapped as the ship is. A broken rock shows 6 dots flying out for 4 steps (one burst a step, where
  the step's last break was; a burst is not drawn again across a seam); a lost ship 8 dots for its 20 steps.
- **The title:** 3720 in large digits; two framed pictures side by side, gentle (one large rock, the ship) on the
  left and full (five small rocks, the ship) on the right, with the chosen one in a thick frame; under them the
  keys as small drawn keycaps with their legends and a symbol each (4 and 6 with turn arrows, 7 8 9 with a flame,
  GOLD 5 × with a shot's dots); the ENTER keycap blinking; the best score under each picture (tally under gentle,
  digits under full) once there is one. The title always opens on gentle.
- **The end:** the ship's burst, its dots spread and still, this game's score and the best (as the mode shows scores), and after 0.5 s (by
  the key's own time) the ENTER keycap blinking. ENTER plays again in the same mode; C leaves.

**The screens that still carry words or digits:** the keycap legends (4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, ×, GOLD, SOFT1, SOFT6,
ENTER: each copies the printed key, so it is matched by shape, not read), the name 3720 (digits), and full mode's
score and best (digits). Getting to the game goes through the APPS and GAMES menus, whose legends are words; see
"GAMES with nine games", below.

**Sound** (app_beep and app_tune). Notes are Hz/ms, 0 a rest. Each tick, the steps it ran are taken together:
1. a lost ship: 392/120 0/40 330/120 0/40 262/300, a tune of 620 ms;
2. else a wave's start: 523/100 659/100 784/200, 400 ms;
3. else an extra ship: 784/80 1047/120, 200 ms;
4. else the shield 20 steps from its end (after a step, with the ship in play): 1568/30 0/30 1568/30, 90 ms;
5. else, when no tune is playing, one beep: a thud for a broken rock, the largest broken in the tick (large 196 Hz,
   medium 262, small 392; 30 ms); else a click for a shot (1,500 Hz, 8 ms); else, on a step that is a multiple of 4
   while the engine fires, a soft low tick (110 Hz, 15 ms).

A new tune can start over one still playing. The tick tells a non-reader the engine is on, and it is what the
stand-in can hear.

## The frame's cost and the state, against SALVO

**Rows.** Rev A sends only the rows that changed, about 0.42 ms a row (52 bytes at 1 MHz). In a wrap-around field
every moving rock changes its rows, wherever it is, so this layout costs more than SALVO's. A throwaway model
(probes/rows3720.py; random play over 6 × 1,000 steps a mode, a rock broken on 3% of steps; computed, not
measured):

| mode | rows a step, mean / 95th / worst | ms a frame | steps over 50 ms |
|---|---|---|---|
| SALVO (its blueprint) | 71 / 109 / 131 | 30 / 45 / 55 | under 5% |
| 3720 gentle, 4 large | 85 / 137 / 196 | 36 / 58 / 82 | 14% |
| 3720 full, 4 large (wave 1) | 107 / 154 / 180 | 45 / 65 / 76 | 37% |
| 3720 full, 6 large (wave 3 on) | 147 / 185 / 220 | 62 / 78 / 92 | 84% |

So gentle draws nearly every step, and full drops frames from wave 3 on, to about 13-16 a second at its busiest.
SALVO's clock already handles that: the steps never slow, a late frame's ms go into the accumulator, and a tick runs
two steps when it holds them, so play keeps wall-clock time up to the 100 ms cap. Rev A's bench measures the real
figure. If full looks rough there, one fix is to move half the rocks on even steps and half on odd ones, each by twice
its speed, which halves the rows a rock costs a step; it is not in these rules because it is a guess until measured.

**State.** The game's ctx is 744 B on the host (x86-64, the SDK runner's count; the blueprint estimated about 650): 24
rock slots of 20 B (centre and speed in int32, size, outline), 4 shots of 20 B, the ship, the key mask and latches, the
timers, the draws' state, the bests, and six burst marks for the screen. SALVO's ctx is 496 B on the same count. The ctx lives where the framework puts every game's, so internal RAM stays +0. Nothing is allocated.
Every function's frame stays under `-Wframe-larger-than=1024`.

**Flash.** Estimated 7-9 KB: LAND's turn math, the rock outlines, the tally, the two title pictures and the keycaps.
LAND was +7.6 KB and SALVO +5.5 KB; the budget per game is 10 KB. resources.py static measures it at the unit.

## The WebAssembly gate

It fits. 3720 is SDK C on apps.h v1.2 like the six games: its only imports are app_beep, app_tune, app_now_ms and
app_seed, all integers, draw.h compiled in. So it builds as a .wasm through sdk/wasm's guest shim from the same
source, and its host check plays every app script through the .wasm and the bridge and requires the native frames,
as salvocheck does. It ships native like the others; games-wasm-gate.list is the STU_WASM build's list and is never
shipped. If it were ever shipped as WebAssembly, decision 68's figures for Babal are the guide: about 110 KB of .aot
in flash, about 0.5 MB of PSRAM while it runs, 2.5 KB of the calc task's stack, and its draw about 1.7 times
native, which would matter here because full mode is already the busiest frame.

## What it saves

16 bytes: '7', version 1, the best gentle score (u32, rocks), the best full score (u32), the games started (u32),
little-endian, then two zeros. Taken whole or not at all: short, the wrong magic or version, or a non-zero byte in the
last two, and it is not taken. The bests update in memory as the score passes them; the framework stores the save
when the app exits, and only if it changed. The mode is not saved: the title always opens on gentle.

## Where it lives

`games/3720/rocks_logic.{h,c}`: the field, the step and the hits, no draw or framework calls.
`games/3720/rocks_app.c`: the app_def, the keys' mask and latches, the screens and the sound.
`games/3720/stu32-game.txt`: name 3720, folder GAMES, MIT. `tests/rocks_trace.c` prints what the oracle prints.
(The C names say rocks, since a C identifier can't start with a digit.)

## The oracle (written before the game)

`oracle/rocks/`, Python, from this file alone, by a fresh agent that never sees the C (SALVO's way):

1. **The model** (`model.py`): the rules above. The C prints the same state after every step (the ship, its shield
   and timers, each shot, each slot, the score, ships, wave and the play clock) for fuzzed key scripts over many
   seeds and both modes, with held keys, taps shorter than a step, turn and thrust and fire held together, and both
   turns at once.
2. **Long games** (`pilot.py`): an autopilot that turns toward the nearest rock, fires, and thrusts away from a rock
   closing on it, in the model and the C, for several seeds in each mode: gentle's free first minute (a ship lost at
   play-clock 1,199 is free, at 1,200 it costs), waves to 6 large, every slot full at once, extra ships and their cap.
   The two traces must be identical.
3. **The rules at their edges**, by hand: a centre at 0 and 399 px and at the y seam; a distance across each seam;
   a hit at exactly r and just inside; a shot that only its first half meets; a fourth (gentle) and fifth (full) shot
   refused; the cooldown at 0 and 1; a split into the last empty slot; a wave's rock moved off the ship; drag on a
   speed under 32 Q; the cap; the shield's last step; a lost ship on a wave's clearing step; the pause.
4. **The app on the SDK runner**: scripted games in both modes with frame hashes and the ticks' asks, the title's
   choice, the save, every tone and tune, ENTER partway through a tick, ticks of 50, 60, 99, 100 and 250 ms, damaged
   saves refused. The same through the .wasm and the bridge.
5. **Plants**, each of which must go red: among them a key left out of each job, the latch dropped, the turn's
   repeat at 1 step, drag rounded the wrong way, a shot tested only at its full move, a split into a used slot, the
   seam's distance taken the long way, the first minute's boundary off by one, the shield ignored, the extra-ship cap,
   the save's zero bytes ignored.

## On the board

- **Stand-in (soroban's board, a window as always):** `device/standin/3720.txt` reaches 3720 through APPS and GAMES,
  checks its name, and plays gentle with keys held as Michael's layout pairs them. Like 077's gate, it tells a live
  key from a dead one by the tones and the timing, with a control held on + (no job): GOLD held gives the shot clicks
  and + held none; 8 held gives the engine's ticks every 200 ms and + none; GOLD with 8 with 4 gives both sounds. Then
  C, and the X=4/X=5 lines. `scripts/device_rocks.sh` (5729ff6's shape: a named, capped, timed container) runs the app
  scripts on the S3 and compares the frames with the host's, with a control that must fail.
- **Rev A:** the frame time, the rows changed and the time between frames for a scripted full-mode wave 3; and the
  **rollover**: Michael presses board 0005 while abacus logs, as for Babal (#8604). The holds to log: every fire key
  (GOLD, 5, ×) with every thrust key (7, 8, 9), 9 pairs, and each of those with 4 and with 6, 18 triples. The fire and
  thrust keys sit on two matrix rows (GOLD 4 5 6 × on one, 7 8 9 on the one above), so most triples put two keys on
  one row and two in one column. The diodes make that safe by construction (work/018); the log is the measurement.

## GAMES with nine games

GAMES shows five games a page, then more (072). Appended in games.list order, page 1 stays BABAL, AIM, LAND, SNAKE,
MINES and page 2 becomes SALVO, 3720, the mole game and CATCH; more wraps to page 1. Nothing in the existing device
scripts moves. The other order puts the three new games first (page 1: 3720, the mole game, CATCH, BABAL, AIM), so
a young player finds them without paging, but it moves every existing game's key path in its device script and
app_test's GAMES block. Michael ruled append (abacus #10280).
